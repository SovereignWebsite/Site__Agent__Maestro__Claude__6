# Draw the commerce line at liability

**The line falls where legal or financial liability starts.** A static or sovereign site (HTML and JS from git, served from a CDN, run by Claude agents) should own everything that differentiates a shop: catalogue data, copy, editorial content, design, page speed, the browser cart and the legal pages. It should buy everything that carries certification, cross-merchant data or tax liability: card capture and 3-D Secure, fraud scoring, tax calculation and filing, and, for digital goods sold across the EU, a merchant of record. Full Shopify only becomes the better answer once the shop needs authoritative stock and order operations across several channels, a non-technical team working in an admin, a big app ecosystem, or the built-in exposure to AI shopping agents that Shopify now gives its merchants by default. On published UK list prices, a static site with a hosted Stripe or Mollie checkout costs noticeably less per order than Shopify Basic at small volume. It also keeps the merchant on the lightest PCI questionnaire (SAQ A) and avoids Shopify's 2% surcharge for using another gateway. The catch is that a "pure static" shop is really static plus one small serverless function, because creating checkout sessions and receiving signed webhooks need a secret key and a server endpoint. For digital goods, the merchant-of-record route is attractive but narrow: every MoR checked refuses physical goods. Lemon Squeezy closes on 31 March 2027, and the agentic commerce protocols all keep the merchant as the seller. Everything below was checked on 27 November 2026. Where a point is my own analysis rather than a sourced fact, the text says so.

**How to read this report.** Every vendor page and source was checked on **2026-11-27**, unless the text gives another date for when the source was published. Secondary sources (blogs, comparison sites, vendor marketing about competitors) are labelled.

## Sixteen layers, and only the experience layers belong in git

The research mapped a shop into sixteen layers. The sourced anchor is payments. Stripe is "certified annually by an independent PCI Qualified Security Assessor (QSA) as a PCI Level 1 Service Provider". A merchant that handles raw card data "might be required to meet **more than 300 security controls** in PCI DSS", whereas card data sent "directly to Stripe without it passing through your servers" keeps the merchant's obligations low ([Stripe integration security guide](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide), checked 2026-11-27). Fraud scoring, tax tables and email reputation are bought for the same structural reason: they depend on scale that no single small merchant has. On the other side, evidence on where shops actually win points to the experience layer. McKinsey puts typical personalisation lift at **10 to 15 percent of revenue**, mainly from recommendations and triggered communications, though this is global data from 2021 ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-value-of-getting-personalization-right-or-wrong-is-multiplying), checked 2026-11-27). Even VTEX, a vendor critical of composable stacks, says MACH architecture is "a baseline expectation rather than a differentiator" ([VTEX, 25 March 2025](https://vtex.com/en-us/blog/mach-mirage)).

*Analysis:* the layers that differentiate are exactly the ones that can live as files in git and be served from a CDN. The layers that need servers and certificates are commodities. That alignment is the core case for the sovereign model. The commodity or differentiator labels below are my own synthesis: no analyst source (Gartner, Forrester) classifying each layer was found.

*Analysis:* the minimum server-side footprint of a static shop is two jobs. It must create a payment or checkout session with a secret key, and it must receive webhooks whose signatures it verifies. Stripe says webhook endpoints need TLS and the merchant "must also verify webhook signatures" ([Stripe security guide](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide)). Both jobs disappear if the shop uses provider-hosted Payment Links configured in the dashboard, which keeps the order record in the PSP. That is how the sovereign model can honestly run with no server, at the cost of having no multi-item cart. Where a function is used, its secrets must stay in the function. The notes did not research how the "encrypted vault" part of the sovereign model would hold or inject those secrets, so that design remains open.

## A hosted redirect keeps PCI, SCA and VAT effort near zero

Compliance is the strongest argument for buying, and it points the same way across every regime examined. For PCI, the January 2025 SAQ A for PCI DSS v4.0.1 removed requirements **6.4.3 and 11.6.1** (script inventory and tamper detection). In their place is an eligibility criterion: merchants must "confirm their site is not susceptible to attacks from scripts". The revised SAQ A took effect on **31 March 2025** ([PCI SSC blog](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/important-updates-announced-for-merchants-validating-to-self-assessment-questionnaire-a), checked 2026-11-27). PCI SSC FAQ 1588 says this criterion applies only to merchants that embed a provider's form, such as an iframe. It "does not apply to merchants that redirect customers to a TPSP or fully outsource payment functions" ([PCI SSC FAQ 1588](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1588)). TrustedSec, a consultancy, warns that the criterion is subjective. QSAs disagree on whether "site" means the whole site or only the payment page, and a merchant who fails it drops to SAQ A-EP, at **about 128 e-commerce requirements** ([TrustedSec](https://trustedsec.com/blog/the-hidden-trap-in-the-pci-dss-saq-a-changes)). The "about 128" figure is TrustedSec's, not PCI SSC's, and the SAQ PDFs themselves were not fetched.

*Analysis:* this gives a clear ranking for a static site. The lightest option is a link or redirect to hosted checkout. Next is an embedded iframe or Payment Element on a page with a strict Content Security Policy, no other third-party scripts, and written PSP assurance. Riskiest is any page whose own JavaScript builds or alters the payment form. Static hosting has an unusual advantage here: the script inventory and change control that 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 demand come almost free from git history and build hashes. That is my reasoning, not a PCI SSC statement. The notes also did not confirm whether Stripe or Adyen issue the formal "script attack" assurance that FAQ 1588 allows, so a merchant should ask.

The other regimes reinforce the same split. SCA under PSD2 is met by the PSP's 3DS flow; Stripe Checkout "automatically handles SCA requirements". For a 3DS-authenticated payment disputed as fraud, liability "typically shifts from you to the card issuer" ([Stripe SCA guide](https://docs.stripe.com/strong-customer-authentication)). The FCA's final UK e-commerce SCA deadline was **14 March 2022** ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/deadline-extension-strong-customer-authentication)). For VAT, digital services sold to EU consumers are taxed where the consumer is located, and the seller must keep location evidence, which a PSP can supply ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers), last updated 28 March 2022, and still naming the outdated "Non-Union VAT MOSS"). The EU's €10,000 threshold applies only to suppliers established in one Member State ([European Commission OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en)). *Inference to verify with HMRC or the Commission before stating as fact:* a UK seller of digital services owes EU VAT from the first EU consumer sale. For goods, UK consignments of **£135 or less** sold to Great Britain consumers by overseas sellers carry VAT at the point of sale ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-directly-to-customers-in-the-uk)), and EU IOSS covers imports not exceeding **€150** (European Commission, above).

Two compliance layers stay on the build side. Under regulation 37 of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, a seller of digital content may start supply within the cancellation period only with the consumer's express consent and their acknowledgement that the right to cancel is lost ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37)). That means a checkbox the merchant owns and must record. On cookies, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added an analytics exception to PECR consent, but commentators say default Google Analytics may not qualify ([Clifford Chance, Sept 2025](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/09/uk-ico-s-updated-guidance-for-new-exceptions-to-cookie-consents-.html); [Lewis Silkin, Nov 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/11/11/ico-sets-the-record-straight-on-storage-and-access-technologies-102ltyf)). These are secondary summaries; the ICO page itself showed an "under review" notice ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/)). The EU's PSD3 and PSR reached provisional political agreement on 27 November 2025, which would put more fraud liability on PSPs, but the final texts and dates were not confirmed ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251121IPR31540/payment-services-deal-more-protection-from-online-fraud-and-hidden-fees)).

## Shopify sells convenience; its lock-in is behavioural, not data

Shopify's UK list prices are **£25, £65 and £344 a month** for Basic, Grow and Advanced on monthly billing (£19, £49 and £259 billed yearly), with Plus "starts at £1,800/mo". Online card rates through Shopify Payments fall from **2% + 25p** on Basic to 1.3% + 25p on Plus. Any third-party gateway adds a Shopify fee of **2%, 1%, 0.6% or 0.2%** on top of that gateway's own charges, and a tax calculation fee of 0.25% (0.15% on Plus) applies "after introductory discount" ([Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing), checked 2026-11-27). The fetched extract did not say whether UK prices include VAT. Apps are the large unknown. One Storeleads-based analysis found only **1.8%** of 3.59 million active stores with detectable app spend above US$100 a month ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-shopify-app-spend-by-revenue-band-2026)), while other blogs claim US$200 to US$800 ([EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/shopify-app-costs-reduce-audit-stack-2026)). The figures conflict, the sources are commercial, and no reliable average exists.

Shopify can sit behind a static front end. Storefront Web Components or Buy Button JS on an existing site hand off to Shopify's hosted checkout ([shopify.dev headless](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/headless)). Shopify itself says "a headless storefront costs more to build and run than a theme". Checkout stays Shopify's. UI extensions in the information, shipping and payment steps "are available only to stores on a Shopify Plus plan", and they run in a sandbox with no DOM access and a 64 KB bundle limit ([Checkout UI extensions](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/checkout-ui-extensions)). checkout.liquid stopped working for in-checkout pages on **13 August 2024** ([shopify.dev changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/checkout-liquid-will-no-longer-work-for-in-checkout-pages-starting-august-13-2024)). Third parties report that legacy scripts on non-Plus Thank you pages ended on 26 August 2026, but no official Shopify page confirming that date was found ([Consentmo](https://www.consentmo.com/blog-posts/shopify-thank-you-order-status-upgrade-august-2026)).

Data leaves Shopify fairly easily: orders, products and customers export as CSV ([Shopify Help: orders](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/orders/export-orders)). A secondary source notes that metafields are missing from the standard product CSV ([Meetanshi](https://meetanshi.com/blog/export-shopify-data/)). The real lock-ins are the gateway surcharge, the fixed `/products/` and `/collections/` URL prefixes (community consensus, not an official specification, per [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-to-remove-collections-and-products-from-url/4467)), Shopify-only Liquid and extension code, data held by apps, and Shopify retiring customisation methods on its own timetable. Whether saved cards, Shop Pay vaults, subscriptions and gift card balances can move was **not verified**.

## Merchants of record take only digital goods, and agents keep the merchant as seller

A merchant of record is the legal seller. Stripe defines Managed Payments as "the Stripe merchant of record solution for tax, fraud, disputes, and support", covering tax in more than 80 countries ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments)). It costs the processing fee plus **3.5%** of the full amount including tax ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/managed-payments-pricing), via search snippet). GB and most EU countries are supported seller locations, subject to an eligibility review. Only fully automated digital products qualify: physical goods, professional services and live events are excluded ([Stripe eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility)). Managed Payments works only through Checkout and Payment Links, which suits a static site well. But it lacks file delivery and licence keys, according to Lemon Squeezy's own comparison ([lemonsqueezy.com](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/)). *Analysis:* a static digital shop on it therefore needs one webhook function for fulfilment. Paddle charges **5% + 50c** and prohibits "physical products or products that require physical delivery" ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing); [Paddle help, updated 13 April 2026](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle)). **Lemon Squeezy will shut down on 31 March 2027**: sign-ups are closed, migration tooling arrives on 2 December 2026, and it recommends moving to Stripe Managed Payments ([lemonsqueezy.com](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/)). The figures for Polar, Gumroad and FastSpring come only from secondary sources and conflict with each other ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/guides/merchant-of-record-fees-compared); [Paritydeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/merchant-of-record-fee-calculator/)).

## The recommended line, tier by tier

*Analysis throughout this section:* the tiers are my synthesis of the sourced facts above, not a recommendation found in any source. The thresholds are judgement calls, so test them against each customer's real order mix.

| Tier | Profile | Recommended line | Build (static, in git, Claude-managed) | Buy (commodity) | Move to Shopify when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Small digital shop** | E-books, courses, software, downloads; UK and EU consumers | **Static site + merchant of record** | Catalogue, content, landing pages, reg. 37 consent wording, one webhook function for file delivery or licences | Stripe Managed Payments (3.5% + processing) or Paddle (5% + 50c; whether it delivers files was not verified); tax, fraud and disputes come with it | Rarely. Consider plain Stripe Checkout with Stripe Tax instead of an MoR if sales are UK-only (saves about 3 points) |
| **2. Small physical-goods shop** | Tens to low hundreds of SKUs, mainly UK buyers, simple shipping, technical owner or agent | **Static site + hosted payment checkout** | Catalogue JSON, product pages, browser cart, shipping-rate tables, policy pages, approximate stock | Stripe Checkout (one serverless function for sessions and webhooks) or Payment Links (no server); Mollie for cheaper UK cards; Snipcart for a cart with no function; Stripe Tax if selling to the EU; 3DS and default fraud screening via the PSP | Stock must be authoritative across channels or locations; POS needed; non-technical staff need an admin; returns and OMS volume grows; you want agentic channels without building them |
| **2b. Bridge** | Tier 2 shop that wants Shopify's back office but keeps its own site | **Static front end + Shopify checkout** | Static pages with Buy Button or Storefront Web Components | Shopify Basic (£25) with Shopify Payments | Checkout core-step customisation needed (Plus, from £1,800) |
| **3. Larger shop** | Multi-market, multi-location stock, a team, sales above about £13k a month | **Shopify (Grow or Advanced), possibly headless; or a headless engine if data ownership is a hard requirement** | Headless static front end and content for speed and SEO control | Shopify Payments (to avoid the gateway surcharge); apps chosen sparingly; or Commerce Layer, Medusa Cloud or Saleor Cloud with a PSP and a tax engine | Already there. Prefer self-hosted Medusa or Saleor only if owning the order database outweighs running Postgres and an always-on service |

Several rules hold across every tier. Always buy payments, 3DS and fraud. Always own the catalogue, content and URLs in git, because URLs are one of Shopify's real lock-ins and the cheapest thing to keep sovereign. Keep the checkout page free of analytics and tag managers, which are the main threat to SAQ A eligibility. Publish structured product data for AI discovery rather than building to any one agent protocol. And do not start anything new on Lemon Squeezy.

## What the evidence does not settle

Several caveats from the research carry over. Radar's price is contradicted between two Stripe pages. App spend on Shopify has no reliable average, only conflicting vendor figures. Worldpay, Checkout.com, TrueLayer, PayPal UK and Braintree UK rates are unpublished, third-party only, or were blocked (HTTP 403). The figures for Polar, Gumroad and FastSpring, Shopify's non-Plus script cut-off of 26 August 2026, Amazon's UK status, and the Visa-versus-Mastercard attribution of the Worldline/ING payment rest on secondary sources. Gaps remain on several points:

- the PCI SAQ PDFs and PSP script-attack assurances
- EU GDPR and the EU Consumer Rights Directive text
- final PSD3 dates
- whether Shopify vaults, subscriptions and gift cards are portable
- Stripe's Managed Payments GA date and the UK availability of its Agentic Commerce Suite
- UK/EU availability of Shopify agent checkout
- GBP prices for most headless engines
- hosting costs for the static model
- how the encrypted-vault component would manage function secrets

The McKinsey personalisation figures are global and partly from 2021. All critiques of composable commerce come from parties with a commercial interest.

## Conclusion

The useful framing is not "Shopify or custom" but "who carries which liability". Card data, SCA, fraud data and cross-border VAT are liabilities that certified providers price at a few percentage points. A static site gains nothing by owning them, and the PCI rules now explicitly reward the shop that redirects rather than embeds. What the static model wins is ownership of the parts customers actually see, at roughly half Shopify Basic's per-order cost at small volume. Its git-based change control also happens to match what PCI v4 asks of payment pages. What it gives up is Shopify's back office and, increasingly, its default plumbing into AI shopping agents. That is the one area where the incumbent's advantage is growing rather than shrinking.

The practical consequence for a Claude-managed practice is to treat Shopify as a back-office service that a sovereign front end can plug into (tier 2b), not as an all-or-nothing choice. Start customers static with a hosted checkout. Keep their catalogue and URLs in git from day one, so the move to Shopify or a headless engine is a re-pointing exercise rather than a migration. Move them only when they need authoritative operations across channels. The open questions most worth closing next are the vault design for function secrets, written SAQ A script assurance from Stripe and Mollie, and confirmation of the UK-seller EU VAT threshold point with HMRC.
